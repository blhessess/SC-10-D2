What Are Acids?

  • From Latin acidus = "sour"
  • Increases H⁺ (hydrogen ion) concentration in water
  • Taste: sour | Turns blue litmus → red | pH: 0–6.9
  • Examples:
    • Hydrochloric acid — HCl
    • Sulfuric acid — H₂SO₄
    • Nitric acid — HNO₃
    • Acetic acid — HC₂H₃O₂ (vinegar)

What Are Bases?

  • Increases OH⁻ (hydroxide ion) concentration in water
  • Taste: bitter | Feel: slippery/soapy | Turns red litmus → blue | pH: 7.1–14
  • Examples:
    • Sodium hydroxide — NaOH
    • Potassium hydroxide — KOH
    • Calcium hydroxide — Ca(OH)₂
    • Ammonia — NH₃

Theory 1: Arrhenius

19th century — Svante Arrhenius (Sweden)

  • Acid: produces H⁺ in water
    HCl → H⁺ + Cl⁻
  • Base: produces OH⁻ in water
    NaOH → Na⁺ + OH⁻

Limitation: only applies to water solutions

Theory 2: Brønsted–Lowry

1923 — Johannes Brønsted & Thomas Lowry

  • Acid: proton (H⁺) donor
  • Base: proton (H⁺) acceptor

Example 1 — HCl + H₂O → H₃O⁺ + Cl⁻

Example 2 — NH₃ + H₂O → NH₄⁺ + OH⁻

Broader: works without water too!

Theory 3: Lewis

1923 — Gilbert N. Lewis

  • Acid: accepts an electron pair
  • Base: donates an electron pair

Example: NH₃ (donor) + BF₃ (acceptor) → coordinate bond

Widest definition — explains reactions without protons!

Strength of Acids & Bases

  • Strong: dissociates completely into ions
    • Acids: HCl, HNO₃, H₂SO₄
    • Bases: NaOH, KOH
  • Weak: only partially dissociates
    • Acids: acetic acid (vinegar), carbonic acid
    • Bases: ammonia (NH₃)

Strength ≠ Concentration — they are different!

The pH Scale

  • Measures H⁺ ion concentration
  • Ranges from 0 to 14
  • Acidic: 0–6.9 | Neutral: 7 | Basic: 7.1–14

Neutralization & Salts

  • Neutralization: Acid + Base → Salt + Water
  • Example: HCl + NaOH → NaCl + H₂O
  • Salt: ionic compound — cation from base, anion from acid
  • pH of product depends on strength of reactants:
    • Strong acid + Strong base → pH = 7 (neutral)
    • Strong acid + Weak base → pH < 7
    • Weak acid + Strong base → pH > 7

Real‑World Applications

  • 🧪 Health: Antacids neutralize excess stomach acid
  • 🌱 Agriculture: Calcium oxide (CaO) corrects acidic soil
  • 🦷 Dental: NaF in toothpaste fights acid‑causing bacteria
  • 🍳 Food: Vinegar preserves food; baking soda leavens bread
  • 🧼 Household: Soaps & cleaners are basic

Summary

  • Acids = H⁺ donors / electron‑pair acceptors
  • Bases = H⁺ acceptors / electron‑pair donors
  • Three theories: Arrhenius → Brønsted‑Lowry → Lewis
  • pH scale measures acidity/basicity
  • Acid + Base → Salt + Water (neutralization)
  • Acids, bases & salts are everywhere in daily life!
